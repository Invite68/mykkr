📜 Historical Timeline
Kurukshetra is named after King Kuru, who chose the banks of the Saraswati River to embed spirituality with eight virtues. Lord Vishnu blessed the land — anyone dying here attains heaven. The Kurukshetra War of the Mahabharata is believed to have taken place here. The Bhagavad Gita was delivered by Lord Krishna to Arjuna at Jyotisar before the battle began.
  • c. 1200–900 BCEKuru Kingdom — first recorded state-level society in Indian subcontinent. Vedas compiled here.
  • 4th century BCEConquered by Mauryan Empire. Became centre of Buddhism and Hinduism.
  • c. 590–647 CEPeak progress under King Harsha. Chinese scholar Xuanzang visited Thanesar.
  • 1014 CESacked by Mahmud of Ghazni.
  • 1206 CEIncorporated into Delhi Sultanate.
  • 1526 CEBecame part of Mughal Empire after Babur. Under Akbar — spiritual centre for Hindus, Sikhs, and Muslims.
  • 1805 CEBritish took Kurukshetra after Second Anglo-Maratha War.
  • 1947–presentMajor restoration of sacred sites. Now India's most visited pilgrimage city in Haryana.

Famous Places

🌊
Brahma Sarovar
Asia's largest man-made sacred tank. Lakhs of pilgrims take a holy dip during Somavati Amavasya and solar eclipses. Hindu genealogy registers are kept here. Bathing frees all sins according to belief.
Sacred Site
🙏
Jyotisar — Gita Birthplace
The exact spot under a sacred peepul tree where Lord Krishna delivered the Bhagavad Gita to Arjuna before the Mahabharata war. The ancient tree still stands. A bronze chariot with Krishna and Arjuna marks the site.
Pilgrimage
⚔️
Kurukshetra Panorama & Science Centre
A stunning 360-degree mural depicting the Mahabharata war. Combined with a science centre. A must-visit heritage experience in the heart of Kurukshetra city.
Museum
🛕
Sthaneshwar Mahadev Temple
One of North India's most ancient Shiva temples. The Pandavas are said to have prayed here before the Mahabharata war began. Still an active place of worship visited by thousands daily.
Temple
🌺
Bhadrakali Temple
Powerful goddess temple with deep historical significance. Devotees come from across Haryana and Punjab for blessings, especially during Navratri when the temple sees massive footfall.
Temple
💧
Sannihit Sarovar
Sacred lake believed to be the meeting point of all seven sacred Saraswatis. Bathing on Amavasya or during eclipses is believed to bestow blessings equivalent to Ashvamedha Yajna.
Sacred Site
🏛️
Sheikh Chilli's Tomb
Mughal-era monument maintained by the Archaeological Survey of India. Built in remembrance of Sufi Saint Sheikh Chehli, associated with Mughal Prince Dara Shikoh. Red sandstone Pathar Masjid nearby.
Monument
📚
Dharohar Museum
A museum about the tradition and cultural heritage of Haryana, located within Kurukshetra University campus. Showcases traditional Haryanvi culture, arts, and way of life.
Museum

Major Events & Festivals

  • Gita JayantiInternational Gita Mahotsav — held on Shukla Ekadashi of Margashirsha month. Celebrates the day Bhagavad Gita was revealed to Arjuna. Massive state-level fair with cultural programmes.
  • Somavati AmavasyaWhen the new moon falls on Monday — lakhs (hundreds of thousands) arrive to bathe in Brahmasarovar. One of the largest spontaneous gatherings in Haryana.
  • Solar EclipseDuring every solar eclipse, millions come to bathe in Brahmasarovar believing it frees all sins. The city sees its largest crowd — sometimes 10–20 lakh people in a single day.
  • 48 Kos ParikramaTraditional pilgrimage on foot through all sacred sites in a ~90 km circle around the holy city. Pilgrims from across India walk the complete parikrama route.
  • NavratriBhadrakali Temple becomes centre of massive celebrations with devotees arriving from across Haryana and Punjab twice yearly.
